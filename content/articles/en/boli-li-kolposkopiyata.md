**Short answer:** For most women colposcopy **does not hurt** — the sensation is similar to a [gynecological exam](/en/uslugi/akushero-ginekologichni-pregledi): pressure, mild stretching, sometimes brief discomfort. If a biopsy (small tissue sample) is taken, there may be seconds of pain or cramping that passes quickly.

Fear of the unknown is normal — especially after an [abnormal Pap smear](/en/narachnik/kakvo-sledva-sled-abnormalna-citonamazka) or [positive HPV test](/en/narachnik/pozitiven-hpv-test). A clear explanation of what will happen often reduces anxiety.

## What is colposcopy?

[Colposcopy](/en/uslugi/kolposkopiya) is a magnified examination of the cervix and sometimes the vagina using a colposcope. The goal is to see areas that need closer assessment — after an abnormality in a [Pap smear](/en/uslugi/citonamazka) or during follow-up.

More on indications: [What is colposcopy and when is it done](/en/narachnik/kakvo-e-kolposkopiya)

## How does the procedure go?

1. **Position** — the same as for an exam: lying down with feet in supports.
2. **Speculum** — the doctor inserts an instrument to see the cervix (as with a Pap smear).
3. **Examination** — the colposcope is not inserted into the body; it stays at a distance and magnifies the image.
4. **Solution** — a staining solution is sometimes applied to make changes clearer.
5. **Biopsy** — if needed, a small sample is taken. This is the only part that may cause brief pain.

According to the NHS, the procedure usually takes about 15–20 minutes.

## What do women feel?

| Step | Typical sensation |
|------|-------------------|
| Speculum | Pressure, mild discomfort |
| Colposcope exam | Usually no pain |
| Solution | Cold or mild stinging — seconds |
| Biopsy | Brief pain or cramping — passes quickly |

If you feel severe pain during the procedure, say so immediately — the doctor can adjust the approach.

## How to prepare

- **Do not use** vaginal creams, tampons, or spermicides 24 hours before the procedure unless told otherwise.
- **Bring previous results** — Pap smears, HPV tests, previous colposcopies.
- **Plan calm time after the procedure** — do not rush straight to another appointment.
- **Ask** — “Will a biopsy be taken?”, “When will I get the result?”

See also [Preparing for a gynecological exam](/en/narachnik/podgotovka-za-ginekologichen-pregled) for general advice.

## After the procedure

- Light spotting for 1–2 days is possible — especially after a biopsy.
- Avoid tampons and intercourse until the doctor says it is safe.
- Biopsy results come after a few days to a week — the doctor will explain the timeline.

## When to seek an appointment

Colposcopy is ordered by a doctor — you do not book it yourself without indications. If you have an abnormal [Pap smear](/en/uslugi/citonamazka), [positive HPV](/en/uslugi/hpv-test), or a recommendation for colposcopy, book an [appointment](/en/uslugi/kolposkopiya) and share your concerns beforehand.

Fear should not stop you from necessary care. Most women say after the procedure: “I expected it to be worse.”
