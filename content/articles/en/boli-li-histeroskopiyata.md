**Short answer:** With **office (diagnostic) hysteroscopy** most women feel **moderate discomfort** — cramping, pressure, sometimes brief pain when the thin camera enters the uterus. This is **not** severe pain for most. **Operative hysteroscopy** is done under anesthesia — nothing is felt during the procedure.

Fear of an unfamiliar procedure is normal. [Hysteroscopy](/en/uslugi/histeroskopiya) allows direct examination of the uterine cavity — useful with unusual bleeding, polyps, suspected fibroids, or other findings.

## What is hysteroscopy?

Hysteroscopy is a procedure in which a thin camera (hysteroscope) is inserted through the cervix into the uterine cavity. The doctor sees the inside of the uterus on a screen and may take a biopsy or perform a small intervention — depending on the type of procedure.

There are two main types:

- **Diagnostic (office) hysteroscopy** — examination, sometimes biopsy; usually without general anesthesia.
- **Operative hysteroscopy** — removal of a polyp, part of a fibroid, etc.; in a hospital setting under anesthesia.

## How does office hysteroscopy go?

1. **Position** — as for a gynecological exam.
2. **Speculum** — for access to the cervix.
3. **Inserting the hysteroscope** — a thin tube with a camera. You may feel cramping or brief pain here.
4. **Expanding the cavity** — sterile fluid or gas is sometimes introduced for a clearer view. You may feel pressure.
5. **Examination and biopsy if needed** — the doctor explains what is seen.

According to the NHS, the procedure usually takes 5–30 minutes.

## What do women feel?

| Type | Typical sensation |
|------|-------------------|
| Diagnostic office | Moderate discomfort, cramping, pressure |
| With biopsy | Brief pain — seconds |
| Operative (anesthesia) | Nothing during the procedure |

If pain is unbearable, say so immediately. The doctor can stop, adjust the approach, or discuss anesthesia for next time.

## How to prepare

- **Discuss with your doctor** — whether the procedure is office or operative; whether to take pain relief beforehand.
- **Choose a suitable day** — some women prefer not to be mid-period; the doctor will advise what is best in your case.
- **Bring results** — from [ultrasound](/en/uslugi/ultrazvukovi-izsledvaniya), previous exams.
- **Plan rest** — 1–2 hours after an office procedure is reasonable.

## After the procedure

- Light spotting or cramping for 1–2 days — common.
- Avoid tampons and intercourse until the doctor allows it.
- Biopsy results come after a few days — the doctor will explain.

Seek medical help with unusually heavy bleeding, severe pain, or fever.

## When is hysteroscopy done?

Indications include unusual uterine bleeding, polyps, suspected fibroids, problems with the uterine lining after other tests. The decision is made after [consultation](/en/uslugi/akushero-ginekologichni-pregledi) and often after [ultrasound](/en/uslugi/ultrazvukovi-izsledvaniya).

For menstrual problems see [Menstrual disorders — when to see a gynecologist](/en/narachnik/menstrualni-narusheniya-koga-da-posetite-ginekolog).

## Booking an appointment

If your doctor has recommended [hysteroscopy](/en/uslugi/histeroskopiya), book an appointment and share your concerns beforehand — including if you are worried about pain. Preparation and clear explanation make the procedure more tolerable.
