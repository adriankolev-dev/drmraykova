**Short answer:** An irregular menstrual cycle **is not always a problem** — temporary fluctuations with stress, weight change, or switching contraception are common. **See an obstetrician-gynecologist** if your cycle is consistently under about 21 or over about 35 days, if length varies widely month to month, or if your previously regular pattern has clearly changed — especially with other symptoms.

“Irregular” means different things for different women. What matters is comparing with **your usual pattern**, not abstract “28 days.”

## What counts as a normal cycle?

The menstrual cycle is the time from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. According to the NHS, a common range is **about 21–35 days**. Periods usually last **2–7 days**.

Many women do not have a cycle of exactly 28 days. If yours is stable at 32–33 days and has not changed — that may be your normal.

## When is irregularity often harmless?

Temporary irregularity can happen with:

- **Stress** — work, exams, family events.
- **Weight change** — sudden loss or gain.
- **Intense sport** — especially with a sharp increase in training.
- **Switching contraception** — pill, IUD, another method.
- **Puberty and menopause** — natural transition periods.

One delayed or shorter cycle is not necessarily a reason to panic — especially if there is a clear temporary cause.

## When is it time for a consultation?

Seek an [obstetrician-gynecologist appointment](/en/uslugi/akushero-ginekologichni-pregledi) or [women's health consultation](/en/uslugi/zhensko-zdrave) if:

- the cycle is **under ~21 or over ~35 days** repeatedly;
- the **interval varies widely** — for example 25 days, then 45, then 20;
- **3+ periods are missed** (and pregnancy is excluded);
- irregularity is **new** compared with your previous pattern;
- there are **other symptoms** — heavy bleeding, pain, bleeding between cycles, bleeding after sex.

A detailed review of symptoms: [Menstrual disorders — when to see a gynecologist](/en/narachnik/menstrualni-narusheniya-koga-da-posetite-ginekolog)

## What to expect at the appointment

The doctor will ask about:

- cycle length and regularity (a calendar or app helps);
- how heavy periods are;
- pain, bleeding between cycles;
- contraception, medications, recent lifestyle changes.

[Ultrasound](/en/uslugi/ultrazvukovi-izsledvaniya) or other steps may be recommended — depending on findings. The goal is not “to find something,” but to understand whether there is a cause that needs attention.

## Practical tips before the appointment

1. **Track your cycle** for 2–3 months — dates, heaviness, symptoms.
2. **Take a pregnancy test** if there is a delay and pregnancy is possible.
3. **Write down your questions** — “Is this normal for me?”, “Do I need testing?”
4. See [Preparing for a gynecological exam](/en/narachnik/podgotovka-za-ginekologichen-pregled).

Irregularity is sometimes temporary. When it keeps repeating or worries you — an early conversation often brings peace of mind: either because a harmless cause is found, or because a clear plan is outlined in time.
