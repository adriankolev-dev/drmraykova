Pap smears and HPV tests are often mentioned together — and that causes confusion. Both are part of cervical screening, but they look for different things.

## What a Pap smear shows

A Pap smear (cytology) examines cervical cells under a microscope. The goal is to spot cellular changes — from mild findings to results that need further assessment.

It is suitable during a preventive visit and when the doctor recommends screening based on your age and history.

## What an HPV test shows

An HPV test looks for DNA (or RNA) of human papillomavirus — especially high-risk types linked to precancerous changes. A positive result is not a cancer diagnosis; it means you need a clear explanation and a plan.

## When they are combined

In practice the choice is individual. Sometimes a Pap smear is enough; other times HPV testing leads, or both are done together. The decision follows a conversation — not a generic internet template.

If results need a closer look, the next step may be [colposcopy](/uslugi/kolposkopiya).

## What to do in practice

1. Book a [preventive exam](/uslugi/profilaktichen-ginekologichen-pregled) or an appointment for a [Pap smear](/uslugi/citonamazka) / [HPV test](/uslugi/hpv-test).
2. Bring previous results if you have them.
3. Ask clearly: “Which test is right for me now, and why?”

Calm explanation is part of the visit — not an extra service.
