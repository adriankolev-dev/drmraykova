**Short answer:** After an abnormal Pap smear, an **individual plan** follows — not the same for everyone. Usually the doctor decides whether an [HPV test](/en/uslugi/hpv-test), [colposcopy](/en/uslugi/kolposkopiya), or repeat [Pap smear](/en/uslugi/citonamazka) after a set interval is needed. The goal is to understand what the changes mean — not to diagnose “cancer” over the phone.

An abnormal Pap smear means the laboratory found cell changes that need further assessment. Many are mild and resolve with watchful waiting or a short procedure.

## What does “abnormal” mean?

The term covers different categories — from ASC-US (mild changes of unclear significance) to LSIL and HSIL (more significant cell changes). A detailed explanation of results: [What does a Pap smear show?](/en/narachnik/kakvo-pokazva-citonamazkata)

Important: **the category alone does not predict what will happen** — age, HPV status, and previous results determine the next steps.

## Typical next steps

### 1. HPV test

With ASC-US and other mild abnormalities, the HPV test helps assess risk. If HPV is negative, watchful waiting is often enough. If positive — colposcopy may be recommended. See [What does a positive HPV test mean?](/en/narachnik/pozitiven-hpv-test)

### 2. Colposcopy

A magnified examination of the cervix. Recommended with more significant abnormalities or when abnormal cytology is combined with positive HPV. [What is colposcopy](/en/narachnik/kakvo-e-kolposkopiya) and [Does colposcopy hurt?](/en/narachnik/boli-li-kolposkopiyata)

### 3. Watchful waiting and repeat testing

In young women with LSIL, waiting is sometimes appropriate because the body may clear the changes on its own. The interval and criteria are set by the doctor — not from generic online advice.

## What to do right away

1. **Do not panic** — most abnormalities are not cancer.
2. **Book a [consultation](/en/uslugi/akushero-ginekologichni-pregledi)** — to understand your specific result.
3. **Bring previous results** — Pap smears, HPV tests, colposcopies.
4. **Write down your questions** — “Do I need colposcopy?”, “When is the next test?”

## When not to delay

Seek an appointment sooner if:

- the result is HSIL or a more serious category;
- you have positive HPV and abnormal cytology;
- you have symptoms — unusual bleeding, pain, spotting;
- your doctor recommended colposcopy within a specific timeframe.

## What to expect at the consultation

The doctor will review the result in light of your history, explain what the category means, and propose a plan. This may include a [preventive check-up](/en/uslugi/profilaktichen-ginekologichen-pregled), HPV test, colposcopy, or a combination.

A calm explanation is part of good care. An abnormal Pap smear is a reason for a clear conversation — not for self-diagnosis online.
