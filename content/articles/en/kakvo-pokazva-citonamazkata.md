**Short answer:** A Pap smear shows whether cells from the cervix have changes — from normal to those requiring further assessment. It does **not** diagnose cancer directly; it is **screening** — early detection of cell changes before problems develop.

The Pap smear is part of [cervical screening](/en/uslugi/citonamazka). It is often combined with an [HPV test](/en/uslugi/hpv-test), but provides different information — see [HPV test vs Pap smear](/en/narachnik/hpv-test-vs-citonamazka).

## How is the sample taken?

During a [gynecological exam](/en/uslugi/akushero-ginekologichni-pregledi), the doctor or nurse takes a small sample of cells from the cervix with a soft brush or spatula. The sample is placed in a container and sent to the laboratory.

The procedure takes seconds. Most women describe the sensation as brief discomfort, not pain.

## What results can you receive?

The laboratory describes cells using medical terms. The most common categories include:

| Result | What it means briefly |
|--------|----------------------|
| **Normal (Negative)** | No changes requiring further assessment now |
| **ASC-US** | Mild changes of unclear significance — often followed by HPV test |
| **LSIL** | Low-grade cell changes — usually follow-up or colposcopy |
| **HSIL** | More significant changes — usually colposcopy |

Exact interpretation depends on age, HPV status, and previous results — not on the category name alone.

## What happens when there is an abnormality?

With a normal result, the next [preventive check-up](/en/uslugi/profilaktichen-ginekologichen-pregled) is usually planned according to guidelines.

With an abnormality, the doctor may recommend:

1. **HPV test** — to assess whether changes are linked to the virus.
2. **Repeat Pap smear** — after a set interval.
3. **[Colposcopy](/en/uslugi/kolposkopiya)** — a magnified examination of the cervix.

More on next steps: [What happens after an abnormal Pap smear?](/en/narachnik/kakvo-sledva-sled-abnormalna-citonamazka)

## When is a Pap smear done?

The interval is set by age, history, and national screening recommendations. It is usually discussed at a [preventive check-up](/en/uslugi/profilaktichen-ginekologichen-pregled) — not on an arbitrary calendar date.

If you have a [positive HPV test](/en/narachnik/pozitiven-hpv-test) or symptoms, do not wait for the next planned screening — book a consultation.

## How to prepare

- Avoid vaginal creams, spermicides, and douching 24–48 hours before the exam unless told otherwise.
- Bring previous results.
- Ask: “When should I expect the result and how will I be notified?”

The Pap smear is one of the most effective screening methods in gynecology. Early detection allows calm next steps — not fear.
