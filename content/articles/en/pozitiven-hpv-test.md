**Short answer:** A positive HPV test means human papillomavirus (HPV) was detected in a sample from the cervix — most often a high-risk type linked to cell changes. This is **not** a “cancer” diagnosis and does not mean you already have disease. Next steps — watchful waiting, repeat testing, [Pap smear](/en/uslugi/citonamazka), or [colposcopy](/en/uslugi/kolposkopiya) — are decided by the doctor based on your age, HPV type, and previous results.

HPV is extremely common. According to the NHS, most sexually active people will encounter HPV at some point. In many women the immune system clears it without treatment. The test simply says: “the virus was detected” — not what will happen next.

## What does the HPV test show?

The HPV test looks for viral DNA (or RNA) in cervical cells. It differs from a [Pap smear](/en/uslugi/citonamazka), which examines the cells themselves under a microscope.

- **Negative result** — the virus was not detected in the sample (at the time it was taken).
- **Positive result** — the virus was detected; it is often specified whether it is a high-risk type.

The difference between the two tests is explained in detail in [HPV test vs Pap smear](/en/narachnik/hpv-test-vs-citonamazka).

## What happens after a positive result?

There is no single universal algorithm. Usually the doctor considers:

1. **Which HPV type** was detected (high- or low-risk).
2. **What the Pap smear shows** — normal, mild changes (ASC-US, LSIL), or more significant findings.
3. **Age and history** — first positive result or recurring; previous colposcopies.

Possible next steps include:

- **Watchful waiting and repeat testing** after a set interval — in many young women with normal cytology.
- **Colposcopy** — a magnified examination of the cervix if results require closer assessment.
- **Combined with a [preventive check-up](/en/uslugi/profilaktichen-ginekologichen-pregled)** — for the full picture and a conversation about follow-up.

## When not to delay a consultation

Seek an [obstetrician-gynecologist appointment](/en/uslugi/akushero-ginekologichni-pregledi) if:

- you receive a result for the first time and do not understand what it means;
- you have a positive HPV **and** an abnormal Pap smear;
- the result keeps repeating on follow-up tests;
- you have symptoms — unusual bleeding, spotting, or pain (these require assessment regardless of HPV).

## How to prepare for the conversation with your doctor

- Bring **all previous results** — Pap smears, HPV tests, colposcopies.
- Write down your questions: “Which HPV type was detected?”, “Do I need colposcopy now?”, “When is the next test?”
- Do not search only for the worst-case scenario online — what matters is assessment in light of your results.

A calm explanation is part of good care. The [HPV test](/en/uslugi/hpv-test) is part of early detection and follow-up — it does not mean bad news.
